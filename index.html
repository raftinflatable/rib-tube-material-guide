One Hull, Four Tube Rows: Choosing Standard PVC, Mehler PVC or Achilles Hypalon on a Small Aluminum RIB

A model code on a boat listing feels like a complete answer. On the aluminum RIB pages at Raft Inflatable, it is only the start. Raft Inflatable is the public export catalog of Weihai Reachsea Outdoors Co., Ltd., and its model pages for the UL280 and the UT360 each show several catalog rows under one model name. The rows share a hull and differ in tube material and thickness. This article explains how to read those rows and how to turn the choice into a request for quotation that a factory can answer without guessing.

UT360 aluminum RIB catalog page visual from the Reachsea PDF. Image: raftinflatable.com.

What the two model pages actually record

Both pages present a catalog specification snapshot taken from the supplied Reachsea factory catalog. The site is explicit that these values identify a shortlist model and are not a final production specification.

  • UL280: 280 cm length, 163 cm beam, 15 hp maximum, 4 persons. Catalog construction is listed as PVC or Hypalon-style tube with an aluminum hull.
  • UT360: 360 cm length, 163 cm beam, 25 hp maximum, 6 persons. Catalog construction is an aluminum hull with locker options.

The UT360 page also places the model beside the A360, a 360 cm advanced aluminum RIB with a 180 cm beam, 40 hp maximum and a 5-person rating. The page asks a direct question: does the buyer need the UT locker layout or the A360 console-ready option? The two share a length, but they are adjacent catalog rows, not interchangeable finished boats.

Neither page shows a price, and each carries the same two notices: commercial terms require a written RFQ, and there is no certification claim, so order-specific documents have to be requested.

The site adds a sentence that matters: these are catalog alternatives, not upgrades included in one boat. A buyer who writes "UT360, Hypalon" in an email has named a material family. A buyer who writes "UT360, row no.21r" has named a specific line in a specific document. Only the second can be checked against a build sheet.

Why the row number beats the material name

There are three practical reasons to quote the row.

First, thickness travels with the row. The UT360 has two Standard PVC rows, 0.9 mm and 1.2 mm. "Standard PVC" alone leaves the thickness open, and two suppliers could quote different builds against the same words.

Second, the site uses careful wording elsewhere. On many catalog pages Raft Inflatable writes "Hypalon-style tube" rather than "Hypalon". The UL280 and UT360 row tables name "Achilles Hypalon" as catalog wording. Neither form is a certification or a material test. Treat it as source wording to be confirmed in the written specification and supported by whatever material documents the order requires.

Third, price and old contact details have been physically removed from the excerpts. The rows exist to identify configuration, not to set commercial terms. Any comparison of material options has to happen inside one written quotation that names each row.

Turning the choice into an RFQ

Each model page ends with the same list of things a usable quote must confirm:

  1. Tube and hull material
  2. Motor and payload requirement
  3. Console, floor and accessory scope
  4. MOQ, packing and destination port
  5. Inspection, test and document requirements

A clean way to use the rows is to ask for two or three of them priced side by side in the same quotation, with identical quantity, packing and delivery terms. That keeps the material difference visible instead of buried in other changes. If locker layout matters, say so for the UT360 and say whether the A360 console-ready option should be quoted as an alternative.

Ask for a matching build sheet for the selected row. The site states that configuration images are sourcing references, not proof of the current batch, option package, production slot or certification. The illustration on the UT360 page is labeled AI-assisted and based on the catalog family silhouette, not a photo of that exact model or build.

What the site does not tell you

Being clear about gaps avoids false assumptions. The Raft Inflatable pages do not publish prices, minimum order quantities, lead times, warranty length or certifications for these models. They also do not give inflation pressures or material test data. Each of those belongs in the written quotation and order documents. If a regulator, insurer or end customer needs specific evidence, list it in the RFQ so the supplier can say what can be supplied before an order is placed.

FAQ

Is Achilles Hypalon included as standard on the UT360?

No. The site lists it as a separate catalog row (no.21r). The rows are alternatives, and the selected row has to be named in the RFQ.

Are the UT360 and A360 the same boat with different consoles?

The site treats them as adjacent catalog rows with different beam, maximum power and capacity values: 163 cm, 25 hp and 6 persons against 180 cm, 40 hp and 5 persons. Compare the recorded values and request the exact layout in writing.

Does the catalog row prove what will be built?

No. Raft Inflatable says catalog values are not a final production specification. Request a build sheet and order-specific evidence for the selected row.

Where are prices for each material?

None are published. Commercial terms require a written RFQ through the request-quote page.
